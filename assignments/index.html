Assignments & Assessment

What you’ll do and how you’ll be evaluated

This course is structured around five complementary forms of assessment. Quizzes and the final exam check your mastery of the textbook material. In-class writing prompts ensure you stay engaged with each day’s reading. The article presentation gives you a chance to lead discussion on contemporary scholarship. And participation rewards consistent, prepared engagement throughout the four-week intensive.

Quizzes (30%)

Three quizzes given at the end of class on Fridays of weeks 1, 2, and 3.

Format: Multiple choice and short answer · ~30 minutes · in class Coverage: That week’s chapters and assigned articles

Article Presentation & Discussion (10%)

Each student signs up on Day 1 to present and lead discussion on one scholarly article over the course of the term.

Format: 15–20 minutes total · summary + discussion facilitation Sign-up: Day 1 (Monday, July 6)

Participation & Engagement (20%)

Active engagement is essential in an intensive online format. Participation is evaluated weekly based on:

  • Attending class sessions prepared to discuss the assigned readings
  • Contributing thoughtful comments and questions during lecture and discussion
  • Engaging substantively with peers’ article presentations
  • Participating in small-group exercises and chat-based activities

Given the compressed format, missing even one class represents significant lost participation. More than one unexcused absence will negatively impact your participation grade.

What about take-home papers?

This course does not use reflection papers, policy memos, or other take-home writing assignments. All graded writing happens in class. The pace of a four-week intensive — combined with the practical realities of generative AI — makes in-class assessment a better fit for this course’s goals.
