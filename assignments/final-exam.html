Final Exam

Cumulative exam · Friday, July 31

Overview

The final exam is cumulative and covers the entire course. It counts for 25% of your final grade and is given in class on the last day of the term, Friday, July 31, during the regular meeting time (8:10–10:20 AM Eastern).

A study guide will be distributed via Canvas no later than Monday, July 27.

Format

The exam has three sections:

Section I — Multiple choice (~30%)

Questions test recognition of key terms, theoretical frameworks, and arguments across the full set of textbook chapters and assigned articles.

Section II — Short answer (~30%)

Brief responses (one to two short paragraphs each) asking you to define, compare, or apply concepts from the course. Examples of the kinds of moves the short-answer section might ask:

  • Distinguish between two competing theoretical frameworks
  • Explain the logic of a particular argument and identify a key piece of supporting evidence
  • Apply a concept to a short hypothetical scenario

Section III — Applied policy question (~40%)

A longer response (about two pages of in-class writing) that asks you to analyze a contemporary policy scenario using the analytical frameworks from the course. You will need to bring multiple frameworks to bear and stake out a defensible interpretation.

Preparation

The exam draws on everything we cover. The most useful preparation is, in roughly this order:

  1. Review the three weekly quizzes — they sample the kinds of recognition and short-answer items the final will draw on
  2. Re-read your in-class writing prompt responses — these are your record of how you’ve been applying the frameworks
  3. Re-skim chapter summaries and your own notes from the lectures, paying particular attention to the comparative pairs (society-centered vs. state-centered; ISI vs. neoliberalism; etc.)
  4. Use the Wednesday before the exam to work through the study guide

Policies

  • The final is taken in class. It cannot be rescheduled except in cases of documented emergency cleared with the instructor in advance.
  • Generative AI tools may not be used during the final exam.
  • If accommodations are needed, please contact me and Disability Services for Students well in advance.
