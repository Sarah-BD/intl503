Class Content

Lecture slides and materials

All lecture slides and materials organized by session. Slides are posted before each class meeting.

Week 1: Introduction & The Global Trade System

Day 1 — Monday, July 6: Introduction to IPE

Reading 1: Chapter 1 · Reading 2: None (logistics day) · Slides: View slides · In class: Writing prompt · Article presentation sign-up

Course logistics and the foundations of IPE. We define the field, introduce the Open Economy Politics framework, walk through the three classical traditions (Smith, List, Marx), and trace globalization across four eras from 1815 to today.

Day 2 — Tuesday, July 7: The WTO and World Trade System

Reading: Chapter 2 · Slides: View slides · In class: Writing prompt

From GATT to WTO: the evolution of multilateral trade institutions, dispute settlement, and contemporary challenges.

Day 3 — Thursday, July 9: Political Economy of Trade Cooperation

Reading: Ch. 3 + Bown & Keynes (2020) · Slides: View slides · Podcast: Trade Talks 17 · In class: Article presentation · Writing prompt

Why is international trade cooperation difficult to achieve? The role of institutions in facilitating cooperation.

Day 4 — Friday, July 10: Society-Centered Trade Politics · Quiz 1

Reading: Ch. 4 + Carnegie & Gaikwad (2022) · Slides: View slides · Podcast: Trade Talks 198 · In class: Article presentation · Quiz 1 (chapters 1–4)

Domestic interest groups, factor endowments, and sectoral preferences in trade policy-making.

Week 2: Trade Politics & Development

Day 5 — Monday, July 13: State-Centered Trade Politics

Reading: Ch. 5 + Bown & Wang (2024) · Slides: View slides · Podcast: Trade Talks 182 · In class: Writing prompt

National interests, state autonomy, and strategic trade policy. How governments balance domestic and international pressures.

Day 6 — Tuesday, July 14: Trade and Development — ISI & Neoliberalism (combined)

Reading: Chs. 6 & 7 + Irwin (2021) · Slides: View slides · Podcast: Ideas of India · In class: Writing prompt

Two paired chapters: the logic and outcomes of import substitution industrialization (Ch 6), then the shift to neoliberal reforms — Washington Consensus, structural adjustment, and contemporary debates (Ch 7).

Day 7 — Thursday, July 16: MNCs in the Global Economy

Reading: Chapter 8 · Slides: View slides · Podcast: Follow the Money (McKinsey) · In class: Group work · Article presentation · Writing prompt

What makes a firm multinational, and why produce abroad rather than trade or license? After a short lecture on the basics — FDI, control, and the scale and shifting geography of global investment — four groups reconstruct the chapter’s remaining core ideas from the reading: why firms go multinational, horizontal vs. vertical FDI and global value chains, and whether FDI helps the countries that host it.

Day 8 — Friday, July 17: The Politics of MNCs · Quiz 2

Reading: Ch. 9 + Bauerle Danzman (2025) · Slides: View slides · Podcast: Economic Statecraft & FDI Screening (Skadden) · In class: Quiz 2 (chapters 5–9) · Group work · Article presentation

A short lecture sets up the chapter’s core tension — a global firm operating inside a world of sovereign states (Vernon). From there, four groups each reconstruct one part of the chapter from the reading and report back: how developing and advanced economies try to control multinationals, why bargaining power shifts once an investment is sunk, and why investment — unlike trade — has no “World Investment Organization,” only a patchwork of treaties. We close on the emerging issue of national-security investment screening and CFIUS.

Week 3: The International Monetary System

Day 9 — Monday, July 20: The International Monetary System

Reading: Chapter 10 · Slides: Coming soon · In class: Writing prompt

Evolution from gold standard to Bretton Woods to floating rates. The monetary trilemma and exchange rate regimes.

Day 10 — Tuesday, July 21: Cooperation, Conflict, and Crisis in the Contemporary IMS

Reading: Chapter 11 · Slides: Coming soon · In class: Writing prompt

The IMF, regional monetary arrangements, and contemporary financial crises.

Day 11 — Thursday, July 23: Monetary & Exchange-Rate Politics — Society-Centered

Reading: Chapter 12 · Slides: Coming soon · In class: Writing prompt

A society-centered explanation of monetary and exchange-rate politics: how domestic interest groups shape currency choices.

Day 12 — Friday, July 24: Monetary & Exchange-Rate Politics — State-Centered · Quiz 3

Reading: Chapter 13 · Slides: Coming soon · In class: Quiz 3 (chapters 10–13)

A state-centered explanation of monetary and exchange-rate politics. How do governments balance domestic political pressures against international monetary commitments?

Week 4: International Finance & Synthesis

Day 13 — Monday, July 27: Debt, Capital Flows, and Crisis in the Developing World

Reading: Chapters 14 & 15 (combined) · Slides: Coming soon · In class: Writing prompt

A combined session on developing-country finance: capital flows and the 1980s Latin American debt crisis (Ch. 14), then the global capital-flow cycle and the 1990s emerging-market crises (Ch. 15). The IMF as crisis lender.

Day 14 — Tuesday, July 28: Economic Coercion and Economic Statecraft

Reading: Farrell & Newman (2019); Drezner (2024) · Slides: View slides · In class: Writing prompt, discussion, and debate

An instructor-led session outside the textbook: how states turn trade, money, and financial networks into instruments of coercion — export controls, the weaponized dollar, sanctions, and network chokepoints.

Day 15 — Thursday, July 30: Achievements and Challenges of Global Capitalism

Reading: Chapter 16 · Slides: View slides · In class: Writing prompt, student presentation

Synthesizing the course: What has global capitalism achieved? What challenges does it face? Future trajectories.

Day 16 — Friday, July 31: Final Exam

In class: Cumulative final exam · Study guide posted Monday, July 27

Cumulative exam covering all course material.
